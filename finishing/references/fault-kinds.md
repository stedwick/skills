# The seven fault kinds

Each editor gets one section of this file. A fault is something a first-time viewer, reader or player would notice and that makes the piece weaker as a whole. A difference of taste is not a fault.

For every kind:

- **Report when:** it is visible without a hint, it affects a first-time audience, and it is at least as bad as the user's example below.
- **Leave alone when:** it is a deliberate choice of the concept that works, it is on the protected list (then propose an improvement, never a removal), or the difference is trivial.

The user's examples come from the portfolio-site run (the three finals of the `creativity` skill, run 3). See `calibration/README.md`.

---

## 1. Concept overused

**Definition.** The concept is on everything, so it loses its force. A strong idea should appear where it earns its place, and the rest of the piece should stay calm around it.

**Fixed looks like.** The concept is concentrated in a few strong moments. Between them, the piece is quieter and easier to read, and those few moments look stronger for it.

**User's example.** Page C ("Legal Tender"): "the passport goes a little bit overboard, where EVERYTHING has a fancy passport-like design." The fix keeps the passport style for the ID card and the passport section, and calms the rest.

**Other media.**
- Story: every chapter opens with the same framing device, or every character speaks in the same quirky voice.
- Music: the signature sound or hook is in every bar, so it stops being a hook.
- Game: the level's one gimmick is in every room, so it becomes routine.

## 2. Details do not line up

**Definition.** Parts that should share a line, grid, baseline, edge or rhythm do not. The eye sees it as careless even if it cannot say why.

**Fixed looks like.** Text sits on its lines, edges share a grid, repeated parts have the same spacing, and things that should touch do touch.

**User's example.** Page A ("One Portland Day"): "pieces of lined paper where the lines don't match up with the text." The fix makes the text sit on the ruled lines.

**Other media.**
- Story: dates, ages, names or the layout of a place change between chapters.
- Music: parts drift off the beat, or two loops of slightly different length.
- Game: tiles, collision boxes or UI elements that are a few pixels off.

## 3. Cheap art

**Definition.** An image, illustration, icon, texture or other made material that looks low quality next to the rest of the piece: crude, generic, clip-art-like or obviously machine-made.

**Fixed looks like.** The material meets the quality of the best part of the piece. If it cannot be made that good inside the hour, it is reduced, simplified or removed, and the item goes to the open list with a next step.

**User's examples.**
- Page B ("Timberline Trail"): "the kind of background map with the trees and stuff looks kind of cheap and low quality."
- Page A: "The dog looks goofy." The dog is loved, so it is "keep but improve": make it better, do not remove it.

**Other media.**
- Story: stock phrases and filler description next to strong passages.
- Music: a cheap preset sound beside carefully made parts.
- Game: placeholder art or mismatched asset packs.

## 4. Parts with no meaning

**Definition.** A part of the piece that does not make sense inside its own concept. It is there for decoration, or its meaning breaks when you think about it.

**Fixed looks like.** Every part of the metaphor means something, or it is removed. A viewer who asks "why is this here?" finds an answer in the piece.

**User's example.** Page B: "The winding path doesn't really make any sense." The path of a trail map should lead somewhere that matches the content.

**Other media.**
- Story: a symbol or subplot that is set up and never pays off.
- Music: a section that does not connect to what comes before or after.
- Game: a route, item or room that has no reason to exist.

## 5. Dead space and poor balance

**Definition.** Empty or flat areas that are not deliberate rest, crowded areas next to empty ones, or a composition that tips to one side.

**Fixed looks like.** Space is deliberate. Rest areas feel like rest, not like something missing. The weight of the composition is balanced across the whole piece, including the end.

**User's example.** Page B: "There's a huge empty green space at the bottom."

**Other media.**
- Story: a long stretch where nothing changes, or a rushed ending after a slow middle.
- Music: a gap in the mix (no low end, an empty chorus), or one section much louder than the rest.
- Game: an empty stretch of level, or all the interest in one corner.

## 6. Effect over readability

**Definition.** An effect, style or interaction makes the content hard to read, hear, see or use. The effect may be loved; the content must still come through.

**Fixed looks like.** The effect stays, and the content is clear while it is on. For web text, contrast meets WCAG AA (4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text) in every state.

**User's example.** Page C: "The UV lamp, while awesome and creative, doesn't make things readable, so that needs to be made a bit better." The lamp is protected: keep it, and make the text readable while it is on.

**Other media.**
- Story: style that hides who is speaking or what happened.
- Music: effects that bury the vocal or the melody.
- Game: visual effects that hide the player, an enemy or the path.

## 7. Lost specifics

**Definition.** A generic repeat where real, specific content was, or should be. The piece says less than it could, because the same element stands in for many different things.

**Fixed looks like.** Each item carries its own real content: its own icon, image, name or detail.

**User's example.** Page C: the stamp collection "has the same mountain icon over and lost the icons for each individual app." Each stamp should show its app's own icon or image.

**Other media.**
- Story: "a city", "a song", "a meal" where a real, named one would say more.
- Music: one fill or transition copied to every section.
- Game: the same enemy, prop or room reused where each should differ.
