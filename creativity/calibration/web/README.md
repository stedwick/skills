# Calibration: web pages

The scorer sees these together with the research captures. They are real results that the user judged. Use them to set the bottom and the top of the scale.

## Failures (user score: 0 out of 10)

Three landing pages for a sea-kayak tide app, made by the first version of this skill. The first scorer gave them 7.7 to 8.2. The user gave them 0. Learn why.

| Files | User's words, shortened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| `fail-board-*.png` | "Giant letters GO and virtually no content at all. Not a single thing original about it." | One huge word on an empty page is not boldness. Emptiness is not restraint. |
| `fail-bulletin-*.png` | "Virtually all the content struck out for no reason. It looks like a rough draft." | A concept the visitor must decode, and that makes the page look unfinished, fails. |
| `fail-cove-*.png` | The worst-looking image the user had seen: very poor art. | Crude hand-coded illustration is worse than no image. Use real images or a strong graphic system. |

What all three share, in the user's words: "They all look identical. Super boring. Similar fonts, similar background colors: very boring beiges, whites, and grays." Muted off-white or charcoal pages, small mono labels, condensed headings, and little imagery are the machine default. They score 0 to 2 for freshness, whatever the concept says.

## Success (user's own example)

- **ardia.dev**: a browser for Mac. The user called it "a beautiful landing page". What it does: a full-bleed hero with a pixel-stretched landscape photo and a sun the visitor can drag ("DRAG THE SUN"), a joke headline in huge black grotesque type ("TOUCH GRASS."), a live clickable product inside the page instead of a screenshot ("NOT A SCREENSHOT. GO ON, CLICK A TAB. TOLD YOU."), a confident funny voice, and dense, honest benchmark data with charts. Colour, play, real product, real proof. The user's own analysis of why it looks so nice: **coherence** above all (a painted landscape that goes from morning to night down the page; warm paper, painted hills, brush-shaped section edges, the palette changing with the time of day), paint texture and fine grain, a strong type hierarchy (large bold Mona Sans headlines, small Martian Mono labels, scaled to the screen), real product captures placed on painted backgrounds, large spacing with short copy, and motion with a purpose (a still image while the scene loads, and for reduced motion). Its only fault for the user: too heavy on CPU and memory.

## Middle of the scale (user scores 5 to 7)

The three finals of run 2 (same tide-app brief, skill draft 2). The user: "way better than your first attempt". The judge gave 7.6, 7.0 and 6.5, so it ranked them in the same order as the user, about 0.6 too high. Score near these, not above them, unless a draft fixes their faults.

| Files | User | User's words, shortened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| `run2-chart-7-*.jpg` | 7 | Sand on the left, blue on the right, and the scroll are great. "Nothing ever changes. It's kind of monotonous." The footer is "too much sand. Doesn't look like a footer." | A strong look must still develop as you scroll: new events, new sizes, new rhythm. Standard parts (header, menu, footer) must still be clearly what they are. |
| `run2-hatch-6-*.jpg` | 6 | Good colours, images and fonts. "The menu at the top isn't really a menu." The background images "don't flow into one another. They're just hard-cut, and it looks weird." | Sections must join: a transition, a shared horizon, an overlap. A row of separate pictures stacked on top of each other is not one page. |
| `run2-paddler-5-*.jpg` | 5 | "I love the cartoony look." The day-to-night idea is clear, but "it actually just changes into completely different images and looks really bad." | Swapping whole generated images to show change looks bad. Show change in one continuous scene (light, colour, a few moving parts), not by cutting between pictures that do not match. |

Note: the user named the cards by their position on the storyboard (first card = A), not by our labels. Always label the options in the order they are shown.

## Near the top of the scale (user scores 7 to 9)

Run 3 (skill draft 3): improve the user's own portfolio site at three sizes. The pages reuse their own painted Mt Hood and Portland layers, screenshots and photos; no generated images. The user: "fantastic"; he scored A, B and C "at least a 7 or an 8, maybe even a 9". **The judge gave 6.4, 7.0 and 7.1, so it was 1 to 2 points too low.** Run 2's judge was 0.6 too high. Together: score strong, coherent, original work near 8, and do not mark it down only because each part is not perfect. Screenshots are in `run3/`.

| Files (`run3/`) | Option | What the user liked (their words, shortened) | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A-small-*.jpg` | A small, "One Portland Day": same page, their scene fixed behind it and re-lit from morning to night | "I love that top-notch bar that pops up and shows the sun" (a slide-in menu whose dial shows the sun's position and the hour as you scroll). The dog icon (pixel-art Biscuit). "The postcard Contact me at the bottom." | Change inside one continuous scene. Small, crafted details that carry the idea into standard parts (menu, contact, footer). |
| `B-medium-*.jpg` | B medium, "Timberline Trail": career as a trail map down Mt Hood | "The idea of hiking and making a map around Portland is also creative." "I like the idea of torn paper and going from day to night." | One metaphor from their own world organises the whole page; torn-paper edges join the sections. |
| `B2-print-*.jpg` | B tie, "Portland Print Series" (7.0) | Not named. | The gallery wall becomes the night sky; the visitor's light switches on the city. |
| `C-large-*.jpg` | C large, "Legal Tender": their résumé as security-printed paper | **The user's favourite by far:** "the identification card, and then the UV lamp dark mode button. That's the most creative by far." Also "the passport book, the stamps, the diplomas, and the receipt are all great." | True facts set in real, familiar paper forms (ID card, passport, stamps, diploma, receipt), and one playful control that is also useful (the UV lamp is the dark mode and shows hidden ink). |
| `C2-theatre-goodnight.jpg` | Large replacement, "Now Playing: Portland" (6.9) | "I love the Goodnight Portland footer." | A closing line in their voice that ends the page's story, inside a real footer. |

Lessons:
- The user rewards **concrete, familiar objects that hold real facts** (an ID card, a passport stamp, a receipt, a postcard, a trail map) far more than abstract styling.
- The best single idea was **a useful control that is also a joke** (UV lamp = dark mode). Look for one in every direction.
- Details in standard parts count: the menu, contact and footer are where the user noticed the craft.
- Good ideas from a replaced direction (the Goodnight footer) are worth keeping: the storyboard should list them so they can move into the chosen option.
