# Calibration: web pages

The scorer sees these together with the research captures. They are real results that the user judged. Use them to set the bottom and the top of the scale.

## Failures (user score: 0 out of 10)

Three landing pages for a sea-kayak tide app, made by the first version of this skill. The first scorer gave them 7.7 to 8.2. The user gave them 0. Learn why.

| Files | User's words, shortened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| `fail-board-*.png` | "Giant letters GO and virtually no content at all. Not a single thing original about it." | One huge word on an empty page is not boldness. Emptiness is not restraint. |
| `fail-bulletin-*.png` | "Virtually all the content struck out for no reason. It looks like a rough draft." | A concept the visitor must decode, and that makes the page look unfinished, fails. |
| `fail-cove-*.png` | The worst-looking image the user had seen: very poor art. | Crude hand-coded illustration is worse than no image. Use real images or a strong graphic system. |

What all three share, in the user's words: "They all look identical. Super boring. Similar fonts, similar background colors: very boring beiges, whites, and grays." Muted off-white or charcoal pages, small mono labels, condensed headings, and little imagery are the machine default. They score 0 to 2 for freshness, whatever the concept says.

## Success (user's own example)

- **ardia.dev**: a browser for Mac. The user called it "a beautiful landing page". What it does: a full-bleed hero with a pixel-stretched landscape photo and a sun the visitor can drag ("DRAG THE SUN"), a joke headline in huge black grotesque type ("TOUCH GRASS."), a live clickable product inside the page instead of a screenshot ("NOT A SCREENSHOT. GO ON, CLICK A TAB. TOLD YOU."), a confident funny voice, and dense, honest benchmark data with charts. Colour, play, real product, real proof.

## Middle of the scale (user scores 5 to 7)

The three finals of run 2 (same tide-app brief, skill draft 2). The user: "way better than your first attempt". The judge gave 7.6, 7.0 and 6.5, so it ranked them in the same order as the user, about 0.6 too high. Score near these, not above them, unless a draft fixes their faults.

| Files | User | User's words, shortened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| `run2-chart-7-*.jpg` | 7 | Sand on the left, blue on the right, and the scroll are great. "Nothing ever changes. It's kind of monotonous." The footer is "too much sand. Doesn't look like a footer." | A strong look must still develop as you scroll: new events, new sizes, new rhythm. Standard parts (header, menu, footer) must still be clearly what they are. |
| `run2-hatch-6-*.jpg` | 6 | Good colours, images and fonts. "The menu at the top isn't really a menu." The background images "don't flow into one another. They're just hard-cut, and it looks weird." | Sections must join: a transition, a shared horizon, an overlap. A row of separate pictures stacked on top of each other is not one page. |
| `run2-paddler-5-*.jpg` | 5 | "I love the cartoony look." The day-to-night idea is clear, but "it actually just changes into completely different images and looks really bad." | Swapping whole generated images to show change looks bad. Show change in one continuous scene (light, colour, a few moving parts), not by cutting between pictures that do not match. |

Note: the user named the cards by their position on the storyboard (first card = A), not by our labels. Always label the options in the order they are shown.
