# Direction C2. Large (replacement): "Now Playing: Portland"

Why it exists: the first large direction ("Legal Tender") finished at 7.1, below the skill's target of 8, so the skill replaces it once with a different premise of the same size.

- **Premise:** Alex's site is a small theatre: their own painted Mt Hood and Portland scene is the stage backdrop, the page is the evening's programme and the stage at once, and their career is performed in acts, from the curtain rising to the final bow.
- **Size:** large. Keep only the content, the facts and their voice (and Mt Hood/Portland as identity); rethink the whole form.
- **How the content maps:**
  - **Curtain up (first screen):** a proscenium stage with a deep red (not purple) curtain drawn back, their five parallax layers as the painted backdrop lit by footlights. Big poster-style typography: "ALEX MORGAN in EIGHTEEN YEARS OF SOFTWARE" (the number is true), and below, the billing block with the facts a recruiter needs: Lead Software Engineer · Cedar Apps · Portland, Oregon · résumé · email. A real menu styled as the programme's contents (Act I, Act II, Intermission, Act III, Stage Door).
  - **The cast (skills):** a typographic cast list, "in order of appearance", set like a real playbill (dot leaders, roles), e.g. "TypeScript ..... as the lead", only true skills from the original; Claude Certified Architect as a starred billing.
  - **Act I, II, III (career, told from 2007 to now or backwards):** each act is a scene change on the same stage: the backdrop is re-lit and re-arranged (layers slide like flats; morning, afternoon, night) and a programme page sets the act's facts (company, role, years, what he built), with the real screenshot shown as a projected slide or a painted flat.
  - **Education:** "Before the show" or a prologue: two universities, the two papers.
  - **Intermission (hobbies):** the house lights come up: a lobby with a card table, a popular video as a marquee with its view count, a chess rating, and Biscuit "as himself".
  - **The repertory (11 side projects):** the season's other productions, a wall of small show cards with working links; two favourites given top billing.
  - **Stage door (contact) and final bow:** the curtain falls on "Goodnight, Portland."; the stage door has the contact details, the résumé and the form; then a real footer.
- **Fingerprint:** palette: theatre red #B3202A and deep curtain #5E0F17, gold leaf #D9A441, footlight warm #FFD27A, house dark #120C0A, programme paper #F4EDE0 (only on programme pages, never the page background), plus the backdrop's own sky blues and pines; type: a theatrical display face with character for billing (for example "Abril Fatface", "Rozha One", "Bodoni Moda" at black weight, or a condensed playbill gothic such as "Oswald" only for small billing lines), and a classic text serif for the programme (EB Garamond or Libre Caslon); layout: a stage frame that persists (proscenium at the edges) with the programme pages sliding in front; image method: their own layers as painted flats, CSS-lit (spotlight gradients, footlight glow), velvet and gold-leaf textures; motion: the curtain opens once on load, flats slide on act changes (scroll-driven, stop at rest); interaction: a spotlight that follows the pointer on the stage at the start, menu as programme contents.
- **Learns from:** Josh Comeau (a scene and character with springs), ardia (one art direction, painted world re-lit), Fifty-Nine Parks (bold billing type), Kings Wild (true numbers set in a print form), Mangham (facts in one block), Spencer Chang (a room you are in).
- **Risk:** theatre costume can bury the facts or feel like pastiche; the billing block must make the facts faster, and the type must stay readable. A red curtain must not read as a cliché (use it once, crafted, then let the stage carry the page). No invented reviews, quotes or audiences.
- **Spark:** lens "make the smallest thing the largest thing" (Biscuit and a popular video get top billing in the intermission); domain "a theatre stage plan"; form "pure typography as image, with a typeface that has character".

All directions must obey: static HTML, CSS and a little JS; assets relative (reuse `../../original/parallax/` and `../../original/legacy/`); no audio; no WebGL; nothing animates forever; reduced motion respected; all true facts kept, none invented; contact details, résumé PDF and project links work.
